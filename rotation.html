Common Crop Rotation Sequences

Legume ➔ Root ➔ Leaf

Planting beans (legumes) to fix nitrogen, followed by carrots (roots) to break the soil, and then spinach (leaf) to use the nutrients.

The 4-Year Plan

Dividing land into 4 plots: Legumes, Root crops, Leafy greens, and Fruit-bearing plants. Each crop family shifts one plot over every year.

Cover Cropping

Using clover or rye during the off-season. It prevents soil erosion and is plowed back into the earth as "green manure".

5 Steps to Implement Crop Rotation

A structured approach to transitioning a farm to a rotation system.

Step 1: Analyze the Soil

Test the soil to understand current nutrient levels (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) and identify existing pest or weed problems.

Step 2: Divide the Land

Split the available farming area into equal-sized plots or zones based on the number of years in your planned rotation cycle.

Step 3: Select Crop Families

Group chosen crops by their botanical families (e.g., Nightshades, Legumes, Brassicas) to ensure diseases are not passed between seasons.

Step 4: Design the Sequence

Create a schedule where heavy feeders (like corn) are followed by soil-enriching plants (like peas or beans) to maintain natural balance.

Step 5: Record and Monitor

Keep a detailed map and journal each season. Track crop yields and soil health to adjust the rotation sequence for the following year.
